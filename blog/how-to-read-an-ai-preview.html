Reading a preview

How to read an AI treatment preview

Hebelie Studio · September 4, 2026 · 5 minute read

A Hebelie preview is a comparison, not a promise. The portrait on the left of the slider is your photo. The portrait on the right is the same photo with one described change. Drag the handle until you can see both at once. If you only glance at the “after,” you will miss what actually moved.

Start with a single treatment

The first generation asks for one treatment on purpose. Lip filler, cheek filler, Botox, jawline contour, skin, a brow lift, a facelift, and rhinoplasty each change a different area. Seeing them one at a time tells you which change you are actually reacting to. A face that looks “better” after four edits at once is hard to learn from.

Light and medium are not clinic doses. They are two strengths of the illustration. Light should still be visible next to the original. Medium is the stronger of the two, and it is still meant to look like a photograph of the same person. If medium feels like someone else, that is useful information to take into a consult: you may want less than the picture, not more.

What should stay still

Identity is the test. Expression, eye shape, and the parts you did not select should stay put. Skin should keep pores and marks. If the whole face looks airbrushed, filtered, or newly symmetrical, treat that as a failure of the preview, not as a result to chase. Save the image only if the change is the one you asked for.

Use the second generation for a combination

After the first result, you can generate again with up to three treatments. That pass is for combinations you are actually considering, such as lips with skin, or a brow lift with jawline contour. Earlier previews stay in the strip under the result, so the single-treatment look is still there when you want to compare.

Bring the saved image as a question. A licensed professional has to examine you, choose a product, and decide whether the change is appropriate. The preview does not know your medical history, and it cannot tell you what you should book.
