Questions worth asking before you book filler

Lumière Studio · September 18, 2026 · 5 minute read

Filler is a range, not a single look. Two clinics can use the same word — lips, cheeks, jawline — and mean very different amounts of change. A preview is helpful only if it sends you into that conversation with a clearer question.

Ask who is treating you

Ask who performs the treatment, what their license is, and whether that person will be the one you see on the day. Ask which product they have in mind and why it fits the area you care about. A good answer sounds specific. A vague answer is a reason to slow down.

Ask how much change, and what “less” looks like

Bring the preview and say which part you liked. Then ask what a smaller version of that change would be. Light and medium in Lumière are illustration strengths, not syringe volumes, so the picture cannot tell the clinic how many units to use. It can show you whether you are drawn to a subtle shift or a stronger one.

Ask what happens if you want it reduced later, what the first few days look like, and which changes are your taste versus which ones the clinic would refuse. You are allowed to leave without booking.

Ask about combinations separately

Lips plus cheeks, or filler plus something for skin, is a different plan from one area alone. In the studio, the second generation lets you preview up to three treatments together after you have seen one on its own. Use that the same way: look at the combination, then ask the clinic whether those treatments belong in one visit or in a sequence.

If you want help finding a place to have that conversation, the studio asks for a name, a phone or email, and a city after the preview. That follow-up is about where to go. It is not a booking, and it is not a medical recommendation. The person who treats you still has to examine you.
